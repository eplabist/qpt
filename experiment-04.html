Why a commutator? It reverses the current through the coil while keeping the rest of the circuit connected in the same way. Reversing the coil current helps reduce directional and zero-reading errors by allowing the deflection to be measured in opposite directions.
03 / THEORY

From current to a measurable magnetic field

A current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field. The manual develops the experiment from Biot–Savart's law: the field contribution from a small current element depends on the current, the element length, the angle it makes with the line to the observation point, and the inverse square of the distance.

dB = (μ₀ / 4π) · (I dl sin θ / r²)

For a circular coil, the components of the field perpendicular to the coil axis cancel pairwise because of symmetry. The axial components add. For a coil of radius r, carrying current I through n turns, at an axial distance x from its centre, the field is:

Bx = μ₀ n I r² / [2(r² + x²)3/2]

At the centre of the coil, x = 0, so the field becomes:

Bcentre = μ₀ n I / (2r)

This axial-field expression is the standard result for a circular current loop: the field is directed along the loop axis and decreases as the observation point is moved away from the centre.

Connecting the coil field with Earth's field

In the experimental arrangement, the magnetic field produced by the coil is made perpendicular to the horizontal component of Earth's magnetic field. The compass needle therefore aligns with the resultant of these two mutually perpendicular fields.

tan θ = Bx / B0
B0 = Bx / tan θ

Here B0 is the notation used in the laboratory manual for Earth's horizontal magnetic field and θ is the mean compass deflection. This is the tangent-law step that converts an observed angular deflection into a magnetic-field value.

Physical picture: moving the compass farther from the coil reduces Bx. The resultant field then tilts less away from Earth's horizontal field, so the measured deflection θ also tends to decrease.

06 / PROCEDURE

Experimental sequence

  1. Fix the circular coil at the middle of the platform and place the compass box at the centre of the coil.
  2. Rotate the compass box until its 90–90 line becomes parallel to the plane of the coil.
  3. Rotate the apparatus as a whole until the aluminium pointer reads 0–0.
  4. Close the circuit and adjust the rheostat so that the compass deflection lies between about 30° and 60°. Record the current and deflection.
  5. Reverse the current through the coil using the commutator and record the new deflection.
  6. Average the magnitudes of the two deflections and calculate the magnetic field at the centre of the coil.
  7. Without changing the current or number of turns, place the compass box at a chosen distance from the centre. Record the deflection, reverse the current, and record the second deflection.
  8. Repeat the measurement on the opposite side of the coil at the same distance.
  9. Average the readings from the two sides to obtain the mean deflection θ.
  10. Calculate Bₓ from the circular-coil equation and then determine B₀ from B₀ = Bₓ / tan θ.
  11. Repeat for several distances and plot Bₓ against x.
These steps follow the sequence in the laboratory manual. Keep I, n and the coil radius fixed while studying the variation with distance.

10 / VIVA PRACTICE

Test your understanding

At the centre, x = 0 in the axial-field expression, so the denominator is smallest. Moving away from the centre increases (r² + x²)3/2, reducing Bₓ.
Reversing the current reverses the coil's magnetic field and hence the direction of compass deflection. Taking magnitudes of the direct and reversed readings helps reduce directional and zero-setting errors.
The tangent-law relation used here, tan θ = Bₓ/B₀, applies when the two horizontal fields are mutually perpendicular. The compass then aligns with their vector resultant.
The denominator grows approximately as x³, so the field decreases rapidly. This is why the measured deflection becomes smaller as the compass is moved farther from the coil.
The current I, number of turns n and coil radius r should remain fixed. The distance x is the quantity deliberately varied.

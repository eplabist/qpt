03 / THEORY

How does a grating reveal the laser wavelength?

A diffraction grating consists of a large number of equally spaced, parallel slits or rulings. When laser light passes through the grating, diffraction and interference produce a characteristic pattern of bright and dark fringes.

The grating equation used in the manual is:

d sin θ = nλ

Here d is the grating element, θ is the diffraction angle, n is the order of diffraction and λ is the wavelength. If N is the number of lines per specified unit length, the manual gives d = 1/N.

λ = sin θ / (nN)

For the screen arrangement, the diffraction angle is obtained from the mean displacement Y and the grating-to-screen distance D:

θ = tan−1(Y / D)

Different wavelengths are diffracted at different angles, which is the basis of spectral dispersion and the use of gratings in spectroscopy and monochromators.

05 / PROCEDURE

Experimental sequence

  1. Switch on the electric power supply to the He-Ne laser apparatus.
  2. Observe the diffraction pattern produced on the screen.
  3. Measure the distance D between the diffraction grating and the screen.
  4. For the first-order spectrum, measure the left and right distances y1 and y2.
  5. Repeat the measurement for the second-order spectrum.
  6. Calculate the mean displacement Y = (y1 + y2)/2 for each order.
  7. Determine θ = tan−1(Y/D), then calculate λ using λ = sin θ/(nN).

10 / VIVA PRACTICE

Test your understanding

The waves emerging from the many equally spaced rulings interfere constructively at particular angles, producing bright maxima corresponding to different diffraction orders.
The mean Y = (y₁ + y₂)/2 reduces the effect of small asymmetries in locating the two sides of the diffraction pattern.
n identifies the diffraction maximum: n = 0 is the central maximum, while n = 1, 2, … correspond to the first, second and higher orders.
For fixed grating spacing and wavelength, sin θ increases with n, so higher orders occur at larger diffraction angles, as long as the order is physically allowed.
The laser provides a well-defined, nearly monochromatic beam, making the diffraction maxima clear and allowing the wavelength to be determined from the grating relation.
